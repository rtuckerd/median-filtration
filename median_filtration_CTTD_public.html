The median hull of the diagonal in a product of trees

Random trees on one finite vertex set X, their box product on X2, and the median algebra ⟨ΔX⟩ generated by the diagonal, drawn as its median graph. This is a depiction of the construction in Theorem 4.1 of Chen, Tserunyan and Tucker-Drob, Finite index extensions of treeable equivalence relations are treeable, arXiv:2610.08986.

This app was made using Claude Opus 5.5.

Changing a color here makes a Custom scheme from the one shown (replacing any earlier one), kept in this browser; the list above switches back to any scheme.

Suggested settings

The defaults work well, with 2 or 3 trees. With 3 trees, turn Ambient down to 40% or lower (or off).

To watch the construction stage by stage:

  1. Drag Cutoff to 1.
  2. Press next to it to step through the stages, about one a second, or move the slider yourself.

Click New seed for a different collection of trees.

At cutoff n, the components of the drawing are the classes of Pn from Theorem 4.1. The highlighted hyperplanes separate the components of the previous stage without cutting through any of them.

    Controls

    • Trees: 2 trees / 3 trees; Branching / Paths (the shape of the trees); |X| (the number of points); Difference (from identical trees at 0% to independent ones at 100%, with ‹ › jumping to the previous or next change of the trees); New seed (a new random collection of trees).
    • Drawing: ⟨ΔX⟩ / ΔX only (the whole algebra or just the diagonal); Highlight ΔX (large dots for the diagonal points); Squares (filled in); Color by tree (each edge in the color of the tree it moves along); ΔX on the diagonal (the diagonal points on a straight line); Even spacing (points evenly spaced along each axis); Unfold (pulls apart branches that overlap); Vivid edges (brighter, thicker edges).
    • Ambient: fades in the product of the trees around ⟨ΔX⟩. Vertices sets how visible its vertices are, and Ambient reach sets how far from ⟨ΔX⟩ it extends.
    • Cutoff n: keeps only the edges of each tree whose endpoints are within distance n in every other tree. It then draws the median algebra generated by the diagonal points of each component of the resulting product: the stages of Theorem 4.1. steps through them. Invariantly separating hyperplanes highlights the hyperplanes that separate components of the previous stage without cutting through any of them.
    • View: drag to rotate, Shift-drag to pan, scroll to zoom, double-click to reset. Spin, Top view and Reset view move the camera. The grip under the drawing resizes it, and the corner button goes full screen.
    • Colors: pick a color scheme, or Customize single colors.
